Contractual Framework

The provisions that govern this question

The Contract is a General Conditions of Contract document bearing the Employer's and the Contractor's requirement, executed on the Contractor's letterhead and digitally signed on 24 April 2026. Four provisions determine the present question — the definition of the unit in which time is measured, the definition of the date from which time runs, the definition of the period allowed, and the Appendix which converts those definitions into consequences.

4.2  The mechanism the Contract creates

Reading the four provisions together (1) Time is measured in working days (Definition 17). (2) It runs from the later of site handover and the issue of the letter of intent (Definition 34, Appendix Sl. 1). (3) Sixty working days are allowed (Definition 35, Definition 30, Appendix Sl. 2). (4) Where the delay arises from any of the causes listed in Clause 19.50.0 — including the Employer's own late instructions or drawings, or the Contractor's inability to secure materials for reasons beyond his control — the Employer is obliged to grant a fair and reasonable extension in writing. (5) Liquidated damages attach only to a certified failure to complete within the period as extended (Clause 19.51).

The consequence is that the quantity to be computed is not the elapsed time between the purchase order and the final bill. It is the number of working days by which completion exceeded the sixty-working-day period, measured from a trigger date that the Contract itself requires to be the later of site handover and the letter of intent, and after allowing for the extensions that Clause 19.50.0 obliges the Employer to grant. That computation is set out in the following section.

ObjectionReasoning
The written certification has not been givenClause 19.51.0 permits a deduction only where the Contractor fails to complete by the date of completion or within any extended time, and the Employer or Engineer-in-Charge “certifies in writing that in his opinion the same ought reasonably so to have been completed”. No such certification has been issued. Until it is, there is no occasion for a deduction: the opinion is the constituent of the power, not a formality following it. It is also an opinion that could not reasonably be formed here, because the causes of the elapsed time lie with the Employer, with statutory restrictions and with force majeure, all of which Clause 19.50.0 makes grounds of extension.
The cause of the delay is not the Contractor'sLiquidated damages are compensation for a breach of the completion obligation. Where the period of completion itself is consumed by the Employer's own acts — the late formation of the contract, the late award of the balance packages, and the late and revised issue of design — and by force majeure and statutory restriction, the withheld right is not a remedy for the Contractor's failure but a recovery of the Employer's own default. Neither Clause 19.51 nor Appendix Sl. 10 provides for a levy in that situation, and Clause 19.50.0 requires the opposite course: an extension.
The base, the rate and the unit are each wrong on the face of the Contract(i) Appendix Sl. 10 uses “contract value”; the figure of ₹2,64,99,120 is a Bill of Quantities dated 29 December 2025, and the awarded value of Purchase Order 4800009178 alone is ₹2,85,00,000 basic. (iii) Definition 17 makes the “day” a working day, and on a working-day count the period from the Purchase Order contained 145 working days, not 178 — so even the premise of 118 days miscounts the unit in which the contract measures time.
Determination The amount of liquidated damages payable by M/s. Riya Enterprises under Appendix Sl. 10 of the Agreement dated 24 April 2026 is nil.
